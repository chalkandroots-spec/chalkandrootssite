Declarative SentencesType 1 of 4

CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.L.1.1.J · Produce and expand complete simple and compound declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences.

Definition: A declarative sentence makes a statement or shares information, and ends with a period.

Success criteria: I can identify the parts of a declarative sentence. I can write one that clearly states an idea or fact.

Examples

K–2: "The pumpkin is orange."
3–5: "Vampires are said to avoid sunlight in most legends."
6–8: "Ancient cultures believed jack-o'-lanterns could ward off evil spirits."

Interrogative SentencesType 2 of 4

CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.L.1.1.J · Produce and expand complete simple and compound declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences.

Definition: An interrogative sentence asks a question, and ends with a question mark.

Success criteria: I can identify the parts of an interrogative sentence. I can write one that clearly asks a question.

Examples

K–2: "Is the ghost scary?"
3–5: "Why do people carve pumpkins for Halloween?"
6–8: "How did the tradition of trick-or-treating begin?"

Imperative SentencesType 3 of 4

CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.L.1.1.J · Produce and expand complete simple and compound declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences.

Definition: An imperative sentence gives a command or makes a request, and usually ends with a period.

Success criteria: I can identify the parts of an imperative sentence. I can write one that clearly gives a command or request.

Examples

K–2: "Put on your costume."
3–5: "Carve the pumpkin carefully with an adult's help."
6–8: "Research the origins of a Halloween tradition before presenting it."

Practice frames

  1. ___ the ___.

  3. Watch out for the black cat crossing the path.
  4. Decorate the classroom for our Halloween party.
  5. ___ carefully when ___.
Exclamatory SentencesType 4 of 4

CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.L.1.1.J · Produce and expand complete simple and compound declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences.

Definition: An exclamatory sentence shows strong emotion or excitement, and ends with an exclamation point.

Success criteria: I can identify the parts of an exclamatory sentence. I can write one that clearly shows strong emotion.

Examples

K–2: "I love my costume!"
3–5: "We found the scariest house on the block!"
6–8: "The legend has survived for over a century!"
