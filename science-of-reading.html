Science of Reading

Three-cueing is out.
Here's what replaced it.

The practice of guessing words from pictures, first letters, or context has been thoroughly debunked — yet it still lingers in classrooms everywhere. I've taught through both eras. Here's what structured literacy actually looks like in practice.

Three-Cueing (old)

Guess the word from the picture. Skip it if unfamiliar. Lean on context to fill the gap. Use the first letter, then guess.

Structured Literacy (SOR)

Systematic, sequenced phonics. Decode left to right, every time. Explicit, direct instruction. Practice to real mastery.

Same goal, one real path

Both aim for a confident reader. Only one gets there for every kid — not just the ones who happen to guess well.

Beyond phonics

SOR isn't just phonics

The Science of Reading has never been only about phonics — it's comprehensive, research-backed literacy instruction addressing the whole child: phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension together. At least 40 states have now enacted SOR laws, and professional development has reached roughly a third of the nation's K–3 teachers. Universal screening matters too — it identifies at-risk readers before failure becomes identity, which is about prevention, not labeling.
